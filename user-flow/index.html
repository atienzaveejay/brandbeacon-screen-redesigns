Open question 1: what the brand actually needs

We have taken “what the brands need” to mean the next post: a brief, a posting window, and the creator behind the breakout. Screen 04 now carries all three.

What we cannot decide for him: whether the shared public page should carry that too, or stay a clean “why this went viral” read. Carrying it makes the page more useful to the one brand and less shareable to everyone else. Ivan’s call.

Open question 2: what the index really holds

One box for brand or product assumes a product search can resolve to a brand. If the index is brand-keyed only, the box still works but the promise is thinner, and we should say “brand” and offer products as a filter inside the results instead.

Needed from Ivan: can a product term resolve to a brand today, or is that a build?

The flow, step by step

Six steps now, down from eight. Desktop and mobile for each, plus the two screens that are variants rather than steps. Click any image to open it full size.

01

Homepage: the search is the page

  • One box, one placeholder: “Search a brand or product”. The Brand / Product toggle is gone, because a product resolves to the brand behind it and the toggle made people choose before they knew the difference.
  • The search bar is now the biggest object on the screen and sits dead centre. The example video moved below it as a cropped strip, so it pulls the eye down the page instead of competing for it.
  • Below the fold: how it works, the numbers, a full sample breakdown, pricing, and three questions. The second image is that page, scrolled.

Why Ivan, 23 Sept: “should say brand and products?”, “anything below the fold? more sales page?” and “for desktop I don’t think it focuses on the search enough, it’s kinda hidden”.

02

Preview: indexed results, and one press to unlock

  • Nothing is crawled while a logged-out visitor waits. The header carries an Indexed Mon 22 Sep stamp and the wall says “already indexed, nothing is being crawled right now”, so a preview costs us one database read and cannot be used to run up a bill.
  • A one minute explainer sits above the locked panel, where someone who has never seen a Breakout Score actually is.
  • Continue with Google is now on the panel itself. One press goes straight to Google and lands on the welcome screen; the old modal is gone for everyone but the email path.

Why Ivan, 23 Sept: “if we’re pulling real videos it might take some time”, “we don’t want to run an actual pull unless they’re signed in”, “maybe an explainer video here?” and “going from page 2 to 4 it seems there’s 2 button presses for log in with Google”.

03

Welcome video while it builds

  • Unchanged. A 90 second welcome plays over the one to two minute build, with progress and the trial terms beside it.
  • This is also the screen an unindexed brand lands on. There is no separate “we haven’t indexed this yet” page any more: the build just happens here, behind the video.

Why Ivan, 23 Sept: “love the video idea”, and “I don’t think we’ll have page #3 … if we don’t have the results, we don’t need to tell them before it processes”.

04

The breakdown, and what to do with it

  • New Do this next block under the analysis: the brief to hand a creator, and the posting window this brand’s breakouts keep landing in.
  • New creator line: who made it, whether we are already partnered with them, and how many breakouts they have had in this category. That is the part a brand can act on the same day.
  • “Why it worked” on its own describes someone else’s video. Everything added here is about the reader’s next post.

Why Ivan, 23 Sept: “is the content on here what we want to share? what the brands need?” This is our answer, and the open question below sets out what we still need from him.

05

Public shared page

  • Unchanged. A shared breakdown opens with no account: TikTok embed, our analysis, credit to the creator, and a button into the preview.
  • This is the second front door, and most people open it on a phone.

Why Not raised in this round. Kept as approved on 22 Sept.

06

My Feed: one brand tracked free, weekly

  • Unchanged. After the trial the account drops to Free and keeps tracking one brand every Monday.
  • Upgrade prompts are about tracking more brands, never about seeing anything at all.

Why Not raised in this round. Kept as approved on 22 Sept.

What we saw first-hand

Walked through in a browser on 22 Sept 2026, logged out and then with a trial account. Screenshots are cropped to remove account details.

Brand Beacon today

Walked, free account
  • You type a brand and pick keywords, then “Run my free search” goes straight to Google sign-in. Email sign-up exists at /register but is not on the main buttons.
  • The popup promises “Your free top-video breakdown stays included”, but Free has 0 analyses.
  • No Share button on a report or a video yet.
  • The 8-day trial collects a card up front.

Kalodata

Walked, trial accountkalodata.com
  • Visitors browse the actual app with every number shown as “$****”.
  • 7-day trial, no card, 15 AI credits. A “3 reasons this video went viral” report took about 3 minutes and 2.75 credits.
What we take: show the real list and hide the valuable part; let the upgrade prompt appear mid-answer without blocking it.

FastMoss

Walked, logged outfastmoss.com
  • Logged-out search returns real data in the dropdown, and the dashboard is public.
  • Shop pages show the headline stats and one row, then “Unlock all 137 products, sign up free”.
What we take: a taste of real data before any account, with a clear count of what is behind the wall.

SparkToro

Walked, free accountsparktoro.com
  • You describe your audience first; sign-up comes second and the query carries through.
  • Report ready in about 5 seconds. 3 free reports a month, no card.
What we take: the founder welcome video (no one else did this), and locked premium sections shown after real results.

Exolyt

Walked, trial accountexolyt.com
  • Two forms, about 9 questions, before anything shows.
  • Then the full top plan for 7 days, no card: “After 7 days, we’ll automatically switch you to our free plan.”
  • Its “Brand Mentions” tab lists accounts the brand itself tags. It does not find creators posting about the brand or score them against their baseline.
What we take: the reverse trial works in this category. Our no-survey start is an advantage worth keeping.

Other apps that shaped it

Walked logged out on 22 Sept, except where marked “from published sources”.

Loom

From published sources

Sign up → record → share → someone views it (the aha) → trial offer

Its data showed the aha was the first view of a shared video, not the recording. Viewers watch with no account; the upgrade offer comes after the first success.

Calendly

From published sources

Sign up → ready-made event and link → invitee books → invitee sees “Powered by Calendly”

A working default instead of an empty screen, and a badge on every shared link.

Gamma

From published sources

Sign up → prompt → AI deck in about a minute → share with a “Made with Gamma” badge

A growth teardown reports most users dropped off on a blank canvas before seeing AI output; getting the output to them fast fixed it.

remove.bg

From published sources

Upload with no account → result in seconds → sign up only for HD

The full result is free to see; only the high-value export is paid.

Similarweb

From published sources

Type a domain → basic report, no account → depth and history paid

The closest shape to Brand Beacon’s search. Its site blocks automated browsers, so we did not walk it.

Senja

From published sources

Sign up → import testimonials you already have → your own wall in minutes

The aha comes from the user’s own data. For us: your brand’s top breakout and why it worked.
