What this version changes, and four things to confirm

Built to your 24 Sept list. The flow now opens with sign-in, so the preview screens from the previous version are gone and the free tier carries the weight instead.

WasNow

Free was 1 search and 0 breakdowns, with an 8-day Growth trial on top.Free is 3 searches and 3 breakdowns, and the trial is gone.

1. The 8-day trial is gone. Deliberate?

Your list describes a standing free quota, not a clock. These screens drop the trial entirely: no card, no countdown, and the three never expire. That is a cleaner story than a trial, but it is a real change to the plan structure and it is not in your message, so it needs a yes.

2. Two counters or one?

3 searches and 3 breakdowns are tracked separately here, exactly as written, which is why the flow needs two paywalls. A single pooled count of 3 actions would need one wall and one sentence to explain, at the cost of someone burning all three on breakdowns of one brand.

3. “Focus on brand?”

Taken as: one input, brand-led wording, and a product term resolves to the brand behind it. That last part is the bit we cannot verify from outside. Can a product term resolve to a brand today, or is that a build? If it is a build, the helper line comes out and the box just says brand.

4. Nothing in the list covers the second visit

The nine steps run from sign-up to paywall. There is no screen for someone who signs in on day two with one search left, and no weekly reason to return. Worth deciding whether that is a later screen or whether the weekly email carries it.

One build note

Sign-in stops a script, but a Google account is free to make, so the wall on its own will not stop someone determined to run the analyses. The controls that do the work are the per-account quota (already in these screens) and a rate limit on the pull. Worth having both in the ticket, not just the auth gate.

The flow, step by step

Nine steps, in the order you listed them, desktop and mobile for each. The shared page is included as a variant because it is where a share link lands. Click any image to open it full size.

01

Landing page, built around sign-in

  • Google is the primary button, email sits under it as the other way in. Nothing else on the screen competes with the card.
  • What you get is stated before the button, not after: 3 brand searches, 3 AI breakdowns, share any breakout, no card.
  • One real breakout sits beside the card (8,637×, a 1.6K-follower account) so the offer is not only a promise. It is the single piece of proof on an otherwise gated page.
  • Below the fold: how the three searches work, the numbers, a full sample breakdown, Free vs Growth, three questions, and the sign-in again at the bottom.

Ivan’s line “Landing page with focus on google sign in (also other sign in), 3 searches, 3 analysis free, also add information on what they’ll get.”

02

Enter a brand, with keywords to expand

  • The first screen after sign-in asks for one thing. Nothing else is on it.
  • Brand-led, as you asked. The question is “which brand”, and the helper line says a product term will resolve to the brand behind it. One box, so nobody has to choose a mode before they know the difference.
  • The expand row is the keyword narrowing: optional chips that filter the pull to a product line. Left empty it returns every breakout for the brand.
  • The cost is on the screen before the press: “this uses 1 of your 3 free searches”.

Ivan’s line “Enter keyword (expand), brand or product (focus on brand?)”

03

Processing, with the welcome video over it

  • The video covers the wait rather than interrupting it. Same 90 second slot whether it is you on camera or an AI voiceover for now.
  • Beside it: what the pull is actually doing, and a reminder that this is search 1 of 3 and the other two do not expire.
  • The screenshot shows a generic presenter, so it works for either version of the recording.

Ivan’s line “Processing + video from Ivan or AI VO for now.”

04

Search results

  • Signed in, so nothing is masked, blurred or locked. Every Breakout Score is readable and the list is sorted by it.
  • Each card carries its own Analyze button, so the next step is reachable from any video rather than only the top one.
  • Export is on the header. Since the whole list is visible now, the thing worth paying for is the written breakdown, not the numbers.

Ivan’s line “Search results page.”

05

Prompt to analyze a video

  • Picking a video opens a short confirm rather than running straight away, because this is the step that spends one of the three.
  • It says what it will do (video, caption and comments), what it costs (1 of 3), and how long it takes (about 40 seconds).
  • On mobile it is a bottom sheet, so the thumb reaches the button.

Ivan’s line “Prompt to analyze video.”

06

Breakdown, with share

  • The four drivers, then the Do this next block and the creator line agreed on 23 Sept: the brief to hand a creator, the posting window, and whether you are already partnered with whoever made it.
  • Share is the primary button and is free on every plan. The counter reads “breakdown 1 of 3 used, 2 left, they do not expire”.
  • The shared page is the one screen in this flow with no account behind it, which makes it the only way a stranger meets the product. It is the extra image below.

Ivan’s line “Video analysis detail page with share.”

07

Prompt to search another brand

  • The prompt does the work of getting someone to search 2 and 3. Without it most accounts stop at one and the paywall never fires.
  • It suggests competitors of the brand just searched, which is the reason a brand team runs a second search at all.
  • The counter is shown as two searches left rather than one used, so the offer still reads as generous at this point.

Ivan’s line “Prompt to search another term.”

08

Paywall on the fourth search

  • The wall names what is still free beside what is blocked: the three brands already searched, the breakdowns already run, and every share link already sent.
  • Nothing is deleted and nothing expires. The block is on new searches only, which is the difference between a paywall and a trial ending.
  • One plan, one price, one button. No second tier to weigh up at the moment of friction.

Ivan’s line “4th search, paywall.”

09

Paywall on the fourth breakdown

  • Same wall, different trigger, and it keeps the video in view so it is clear exactly what was being asked for.
  • The score and the video stay free here. Only the written breakdown is behind the wall, so the results page never becomes useless.
  • Growth is quoted as 100 breakdowns a month against the 3 just spent.

Ivan’s line “4th analysis, paywall.”

What we saw first-hand

Walked through in a browser on 22 Sept 2026, logged out and then with a trial account. Screenshots are cropped to remove account details.

Brand Beacon today

Walked, free account
  • You type a brand and pick keywords, then “Run my free search” goes straight to Google sign-in. Email sign-up exists at /register but is not on the main buttons.
  • The popup promises “Your free top-video breakdown stays included”, but Free has 0 analyses.
  • No Share button on a report or a video yet.
  • The 8-day trial collects a card up front.

Kalodata

Walked, trial accountkalodata.com
  • Visitors browse the actual app with every number shown as “$****”.
  • 7-day trial, no card, 15 AI credits. A “3 reasons this video went viral” report took about 3 minutes and 2.75 credits.
What we take: show the real list and hide the valuable part; let the upgrade prompt appear mid-answer without blocking it.

FastMoss

Walked, logged outfastmoss.com
  • Logged-out search returns real data in the dropdown, and the dashboard is public.
  • Shop pages show the headline stats and one row, then “Unlock all 137 products, sign up free”.
What we take: a taste of real data before any account, with a clear count of what is behind the wall.

SparkToro

Walked, free accountsparktoro.com
  • You describe your audience first; sign-up comes second and the query carries through.
  • Report ready in about 5 seconds. 3 free reports a month, no card.
What we take: the founder welcome video (no one else did this), and locked premium sections shown after real results.

Exolyt

Walked, trial accountexolyt.com
  • Two forms, about 9 questions, before anything shows.
  • Then the full top plan for 7 days, no card: “After 7 days, we’ll automatically switch you to our free plan.”
  • Its “Brand Mentions” tab lists accounts the brand itself tags. It does not find creators posting about the brand or score them against their baseline.
What we take: the reverse trial works in this category. Our no-survey start is an advantage worth keeping.

Walked logged out on 22 Sept, except where marked “from published sources”.

Loom

From published sources

Sign up → record → share → someone views it (the aha) → trial offer

Its data showed the aha was the first view of a shared video, not the recording. Viewers watch with no account; the upgrade offer comes after the first success.

Calendly

From published sources

Sign up → ready-made event and link → invitee books → invitee sees “Powered by Calendly”

A working default instead of an empty screen, and a badge on every shared link.

Gamma

From published sources

Sign up → prompt → AI deck in about a minute → share with a “Made with Gamma” badge

A growth teardown reports most users dropped off on a blank canvas before seeing AI output; getting the output to them fast fixed it.

remove.bg

From published sources

Upload with no account → result in seconds → sign up only for HD

The full result is free to see; only the high-value export is paid.

Similarweb

From published sources

Type a domain → basic report, no account → depth and history paid

The closest shape to Brand Beacon’s search. Its site blocks automated browsers, so we did not walk it.

Senja

From published sources

Sign up → import testimonials you already have → your own wall in minutes

The aha comes from the user’s own data. For us: your brand’s top breakout and why it worked.
